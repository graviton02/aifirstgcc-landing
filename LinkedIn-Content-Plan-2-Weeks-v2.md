### Day 2 (Tuesday) — Conversational
**Topic:** The Firefighting Equilibrium
**Expertise Source:** Firefighting Equilibrium Framework

```
There's a pattern we see repeatedly in GCCs:

Leaders are trapped in what we call "firefighting equilibrium."

It works like this:

→ GCC is created to resolve outsourcing issues or reduce costs
→ Leaders inherit transition problems and legacy governance
→ Performance metrics reward stability (SLAs, savings, continuity)
→ Innovation and capability building get crowded out
→ Leaders spend energy managing escalations, not shaping transformation
→ The system rewards firefighting, so firefighting becomes the norm

The result? Even capable leaders stay reactive. The equilibrium holds.

Breaking it requires more than individual effort. It requires:

• Redesigned metrics tied to value creation, not just stability
• Simplified governance to preserve leadership bandwidth
• A clear mandate to "run the old while building the new"

If you're a GCC leader, you've probably felt this tension.

What's kept you in firefighting mode? And what's helped you break out?
```

### Day 3 (Wednesday) — Educational
**Topic:** The Real GCC Business Case — Value = Influence + Intent + Integration
**Expertise Source:** Value Framework

```
The real business case for a GCC doesn't live in a spreadsheet.

Yes, the Excel model shows headcount, salary deltas, and 5-year ROI.

But that's the facade. The real value comes from three factors:

1. Influence
Can your GCC shape what gets built — or does it just execute what it's told?

High influence: Participates in product decisions, owns architecture
Low influence: Waits for orders, serves as overflow for HQ

2. Intent
Why was the GCC built in the first place?

Strategic intent: Innovation, capability building, de-risking operations
Weak intent: Cost savings, headcount arbitrage

3. Integration
How deeply connected is the GCC to global teams?

Strong integration: Shared OKRs, daily collaboration, career mobility
Weak integration: Feels like a third-party vendor, "us vs. them" culture

Value = Influence + Intent + Integration

No amount of cost arbitrage will save a GCC that's irrelevant.
No AI tools will help if the strategic intent is missing.
No fancy office will matter if teams don't truly integrate.

Which of these three is your GCC's biggest gap?
```

**Expertise Source:** Zero-Based AI Audit Framework

```
Carrying yesterday's structure into an AI-first world is a recipe for irrelevance.

That's why GCCs need what we call a Zero-Based AI Audit.

Not a chatbot implementation. Not a half-hearted AI CoE.

A full examination of what the GCC does, how it does it, and whether it should still be doing it at all.

Five areas to examine:

1. Headcount vs. Output
Are you measuring activity or actual impact?
50 people doing 1x each is obsolete. 10 people doing 5x with AI is the new baseline.

2. Redundancy Mapping
How many roles exist just to manage other roles?
AI now handles reporting, documentation, reconciliation.

3. Skill Stack Obsolescence
Are you upskilling for legacy skills or AI-native capabilities?
The market needs prompt engineers, model auditors, AI ops talent.

4. Process Relevance
Do your processes accelerate decisions or paralyze them?
If governance is built for risk aversion, not iteration, AI won't help.

5. Innovation-to-Execution Ratio
How many AI ideas are in production vs. stuck in decks?
If your CoE is a showcase, not a value driver, it's theatre.

The question isn't "How many people are in your GCC?"

It's "How is your GCC reshaping enterprise value in an AI-first world?"
```
